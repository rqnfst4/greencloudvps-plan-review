## What the price actually costs you over a year

Here's where GreenCloud differs from the big names, and it's the part most comparison posts gloss over: there is no promo-price reset trap.

The large hosts structure pricing around introductory rates. IONOS's entry VPS+ plan is $2/month for the first three months of a one-year term, then $6/month — $60 for year one, $72 every year after. GoDaddy's managed entry plan runs $8.99/month but only for the first three years, after which it renews at $14.99 (that's $107.88/year, then $179.88). DigitalOcean starts at $4/month with a straightforward no-refunds policy, and Hostinger's entry tier sits around $4.99.

GreenCloud's listed prices are the renewal prices. In its 13th anniversary announcement, the company stated this explicitly: despite rising hardware, NVMe and datacenter costs, renewal prices stay where they started, and existing customers are encouraged to keep their old promotional plans rather than being pushed onto new pricing. The terms of service do reserve the right to adjust prices between billing cycles for cost changes, so this is a company commitment rather than a contractual guarantee — but it's an unusually explicit one for a budget host.

One comparison note in GreenCloud's disfavor: IONOS includes a 30-day money-back guarantee and unlimited traffic even on the $2 intro tier, and both IONOS and GoDaddy throw in free SSL and more hand-holding. If you want a managed experience with cPanel and someone to call when things break, those two are legitimately better fits. GreenCloud wins on flat pricing and bandwidth per dollar, and loses on support depth.

## The fine print nobody puts in the comparison table

Several things about GreenCloudVPS only show up in the plan pages and terms of service — and each one can change which plan you should actually buy.

**1. Asia-Pacific plans get less than half the traffic, and extra traffic costs five times more.** The Budget KVM bandwidth figures above apply to US and EU locations. Order the same plan in Singapore or elsewhere in APAC and the traffic drops to 750GB on the entry tier, 1TB on SSDKVM-2, and roughly half across the board — the current sale page confirms a Singapore Budget KVM -2 comes with 750GB instead of 4TB. The terms are equally lopsided on overage: additional traffic costs $3/TB/month in the US and EU but $15/TB/month in APAC. If your server will mostly serve visitors in Asia, do the math on your actual transfer needs first.

**2. The refund policy has more exclusions than most reviews mention.** The base rule: new customers can cancel their first VPS within 7 days for a refund. From there, the terms carve out a long list of exclusions — cryptocurrency payments, upgrades and additional orders, any service that has used more than 10% of its allocated bandwidth, promotional and sale plans, dedicated servers and proxies. Current sale plans state it outright: "No refund/Money back on this plan." So before you click buy on anything labeled as a sale or special deal, assume it's final.

> If a refund option matters to you, buy a regular-priced plan (not a sale plan) as your first VPS, keep your first-week bandwidth usage under 10% until you're sure, and pay with PayPal or a card rather than crypto.

**3. Billing has real teeth.** Invoices go out 14 days before the due date. Pay more than 2 days late and a 10% late fee applies; the account can be suspended 1 day after expiry and terminated after 3. Opening a PayPal or credit card dispute rather than a support ticket triggers immediate suspension or termination of all services. None of this is unusual for the industry, but with sale plans being non-refundable, there's no safety net if you miss a payment cycle.

**4. Fair use limits what the cheap plans can run.** The terms prohibit game servers (Counter-Strike and similar), TOR exit nodes, and streaming or live broadcast on VPS plans. Sustained 100%+ CPU usage beyond 10 minutes isn't allowed, and the port is fair-share: pushing over 100Mbps continuously for 6+ hours or saturating the port repeatedly can get flagged. For websites, VPNs, bots and typical automation, this never comes up. For anything bandwidth- or CPU-heavy, it's the difference between a $6 plan being enough and being a problem.

**5. The sale hardware is a mixed bag, and stock shifts constantly.** GreenCloud's Budget KVM sale page mixes two generations of servers: newer AMD EPYC (Rome/Milan) with NVMe storage, and older Intel E5v4 with SATA SSD. The EPYC locations sell out first — at the time of checking, Ashburn, Los Angeles, Toronto and Ogden all showed zero availability, while Chicago, San Jose, Phoenix, Jacksonville, Kansas City and Dallas had E5v4 stock, some with extra SWAP memory thrown in. Check which CPU generation your chosen location runs before ordering, because the performance gap between EPYC+NVMe and E5v4+SSD is real.
